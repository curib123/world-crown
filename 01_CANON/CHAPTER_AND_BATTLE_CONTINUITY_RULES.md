# WORLD CROWN - Chapter and Battle Continuity Rules

## Chapter Contract

Every finished chapter must function as a standalone dramatic unit and as a link in the larger chain. A chapter plan or draft must identify:

1. one dominant POV and scene-specific voice;
2. an immediate objective, pressure, conflict, or meaningful decision;
3. an emotional movement or changed understanding;
4. a payoff from the previous chapter or an intentional first-chapter setup;
5. an unresolved pressure or changed state carried forward;
6. at least one forward foreshadowing seed for the next chapter or a later reveal;
7. a closing change: decision, consequence, discovery, relationship shift, tactical change, or new danger.

The next chapter should receive a direct handoff from this changed state even when it changes POV, location, nation, or time. A handoff may be causal, emotional, tactical, investigative, political, relational, or thematic.

## Word Minimum

Every finished manuscript chapter must contain at least 1,000 words of meaningful narrative prose. Chapter titles, planning notes, metadata, author notes, and QA checklists do not count. The minimum is a floor for complete scene work, not permission for padding, repetitive exposition, artificial fragmentation, or several tiny scenes that do not form a coherent unit.

## Quiet Chapters

Quiet chapters obey the same contract. Their main conflict may be recovery, political pressure, investigation, cultural interaction, grief, preparation, civilian consequence, or relationship change rather than combat. A quiet chapter does not need a cliffhanger, but it must end with a changed state.

## Champion Presentation

Every battle begins with a Champion Presentation before the first meaningful exchange. Both fighters receive a presentation, though the length may vary with the match's importance. The information must arrive through dramatic action, witness reaction, records, dialogue, memory, public interpretation, or contrast with the opponent.

The presentation establishes, in a character-specific order:

- name, national entry, era, and immediate reason for fighting;
- Crown Title and the historical or legendary idea it represents;
- verified accomplishments, separated from disputed claims and later legend;
- Human Art and recognizable combat method;
- Regalia, Legacy Trait, known capability, and meaningful limitation;
- why this opponent is a physical, historical, ideological, or personal contrast;
- one unanswered question, concealed cost, or foreshadowed danger;
- the transition from introduction into the first meaningful exchange.

The champion must be legible as a person before being treated as a symbol. The presentation is not a biography dump and must not grant an ability without established identity, historical basis, prior observation, or fair foreshadowing. Supporting champions receive emotional dignity even when the match is primarily told through a core protagonist's POV.

## Arrival Variety

The way a champion arrives must be distinct from the neighboring major battles. Record an arrival mode in the match plan and vary staging, viewpoint, emotional register, and reveal order. Suitable modes include public challenge, mid-conflict intervention, silent observation, aftermath discovery, national dispatch, civilian encounter, opponent memory, ritual or record activation, environmental interaction, and refusal before acceptance.

Do not repeat the same procession, announcement, spotlight, narrator introduction, posed entrance, or first-ability reveal as the default pattern. The chosen mode must grow from the champion's identity, history, relationship to the nation, and present reason for fighting. Variation is not random novelty: the arrival must reveal character and create a different kind of anticipation.

## Battle Continuity

The battle must pay off at least one prior relationship, historical, mechanical, thematic, or mystery seed. Its result must create a new state for the next chapter: a tactical change, injury, belief shift, relationship consequence, public reaction, national consequence, Crown clue, or future strategic problem.

No three major fights in sequence may use the same reveal order, flashback timing, rhythm, or emotional ending. Every result must be earned by established Human Art, Crown Body, Regalia, Legacy, Signature Arts, Legend Authority, psychology, strategy, and cost.
