# WORLD CROWN - Foreshadowing and Mystery Rules

## Core Rule

Mystery creates questions before it supplies answers. Foreshadowing must be visible in retrospect and fair in the moment. A surprise may exceed the reader's prediction, but it may not depend on information the story deliberately made impossible to notice or understand.

## Seed Categories

Every tracked seed uses one or more of these categories:

- behavioral: a repeated choice, avoidance, habit, or contradiction;
- visual: an object, mark, image, spatial pattern, or physical anomaly;
- dialogue: a phrase, omission, correction, euphemism, or inconsistent account;
- historical: a record, date, witness, weapon, ruin, or conflicting source;
- mechanical: a power condition, cost, limitation, rule wording, or tactical result;
- relational: a bond, rivalry, debt, betrayal, or changed response;
- thematic: an image or dilemma that gains meaning when the central question develops.

## Seed Lifecycle

The foreshadowing ledger records each important seed through this sequence:

1. seed: the first observable detail or event;
2. first interpretation: the reasonable explanation available at the time;
3. reinforcement: a later detail that keeps the question alive;
4. misdirection: optional competing interpretation that remains fair;
5. reveal: the new information or rule that answers the question;
6. payoff: the earlier detail reinterpreted or used;
7. consequence: the choice, relationship, danger, or mystery changed by the reveal.

Not every minor seed needs all seven stages, but every medium or major reveal does.

## Mystery Scales

### Minor

Resolves within a match, chapter run, or short saga. Examples include a concealed technique condition, a false account of an accomplishment, or the reason a delegation changes its public position.

### Medium

Spans several sagas and changes how characters interpret the tournament. Examples include the Herald's selective wording, a pattern in National Collapse, or evidence that a champion's legend was deliberately shaped.

### Major

Concerns World Crown origin, previous cycles, the Herald, National Collapse reversibility, what victory grants, or the true purpose of the tournament. Major mysteries must affect the final choices of multiple protagonists.

## Reveal Progression

- Opening: establish basic rules and unexplained anomalies.
- First Collapse: prove Collapse and show at least one contradiction.
- Final 32: show evidence that the Crown withholds information.
- Final 16: credibly confirm earlier Crown phenomena.
- Sovereign Eight: prove previous civilizations were affected and the Crown influenced history.
- Four Flags: reveal the one-nation survival rule; earlier wording becomes sinister in hindsight.
- Post-Final: reveal deeper mechanism, ideology, historical intervention, and final-collapse logic.

## Fair-Play Rules

- Plant information before the reveal, not only in the reveal scene.
- Reuse a seed in a new context so it gains meaning rather than sitting as decoration.
- Misdirection must support a plausible interpretation, not a dishonest one.
- A hidden rule must preserve the established power-system logic or explain why an apparent contradiction existed.
- A reveal must change a future decision, not merely answer a trivia question.
- Do not undo meaningful death, sacrifice, elimination, or National Collapse solely for shock.

## Chapter Connection

Every chapter plan identifies at least one seed it pays off and one seed or unresolved question it carries forward. A forward seed may be quiet; it can be a gesture, altered wording, unexplained cost, historical inconsistency, or relationship change. The chapter still needs a local ending change even when the mystery remains open.
