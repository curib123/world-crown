# Ensemble Cast Rules

## Core Ensemble Size
Maintain roughly 8–12 recurring primary protagonists during the early and middle series. This group is not a team; it is a cross-section of the tournament.

## Selection Principles
Vary nation/region, historical period, fighting method, social role, philosophy, relationship to national identity, attitude toward the Crown, and moral complexity.

## Required Variety
Include a mix such as ancient/early historical warrior, medieval or early-modern fighter, modern professional fighter, ruler/commander, rebel/revolutionary, strategist, woman with genuine martial or wartime relevance, lesser-known/underestimated champion, and morally complicated figure.

## Protagonist Status
Narrative investment does not guarantee advancement. Supporting champions may become major later if earned.

## Relationship Web
Each core protagonist should have at least one meaningful rival, one bond based on respect/friendship/mentorship/curiosity, one ideological conflict, and one relationship changed by a match result.

Before a recurring protagonist fights another recurring protagonist, establish meaningful relationship material unless deliberate first-contact shock is the point. Every battle introduction must give both champions equal narrative dignity, even when the chapter uses one fighter as the dominant POV. A Champion Presentation should make each person understandable before presenting them as a national symbol.

## Country Balance
No country functions as the automatic hero nation.

## Historical Personhood
Champions are people first and symbols second. They may disagree with modern governments, reject how history remembers them, or reject modern nationalism.

Champion profiles must separate documented history, disputed history, later legend, Crown interpretation, and deliberate fiction. Verified accomplishments and public reputation are both useful, but neither is allowed to erase contradiction or personhood.

## Post-Elimination Use
A surviving eliminated champion may continue as spectator, analyst, mentor, investigator, dissenter, refugee advocate, Crown rebel, ally, or adversary.

Elimination may complete or redirect a character arc. It must never automatically remove a champion's narrative function when their relationships, knowledge, moral choice, or civilian connection still carries forward.
