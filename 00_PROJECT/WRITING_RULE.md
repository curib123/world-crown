# WORLD CROWN - Writing Rule

## Default Narrative Distance

Write in close third-person limited by default. Stay near the current POV character's perceptions, assumptions, vocabulary, emotional state, physical sensations, and attention. Do not reveal information the POV character cannot perceive, infer, remember, or reasonably misunderstand.

Switch POV only between chapters or at a clear scene break. Mark a shift through changed perception and voice, not an unexplained jump into another mind.

## Chapter Standard

Every finished chapter must contain at least 1,000 meaningful words of narrative prose. Titles, planning notes, metadata, author notes, and QA checklists do not count. The minimum must be met through complete scene work, not padding, repeated exposition, artificial fragmentation, or decorative description that does not affect character, conflict, atmosphere, or choice.

Each chapter must stand alone through a local objective or pressure, conflict or meaningful decision, emotional movement, and closing change. It must connect to the previous and next chapters through payoff, changed state, unresolved pressure, or a forward foreshadowing seed.

## Emotion

Show emotion through what the character notices, avoids, says, fails to say, remembers, misunderstands, physically does, and chooses. Name an emotion when it sounds natural, but do not rely on labels where behavior, sensation, subtext, or consequence can carry the moment.

## Dialogue and Conversation

Use substantial natural dialogue when relationships or decisions matter. Allow interruptions, misunderstandings, evasions, humor, silence, subtext, disagreement, unfinished thoughts, and changes of subject. Historical characters should be readable without everyone speaking in fake archaic English.

Each recurring character needs recognizable conversational habits grounded in personality, era, education, rank, temperament, and relationship. Avoid gimmicky catchphrases and interchangeable voices.

## Rhythm and Compression

Vary sentence and paragraph length naturally. Use cinematic compression selectively for danger, combat, horror, revelation, evolution, emotional peaks, and sudden interruption. Do not make every sentence short, every paragraph dramatic, or every beat a one-line fragment.

## Historical and Combat Prose

History should enter through a present dramatic question. A Champion Presentation establishes personhood, achievement, Crown Title, capability, limitation, and danger through action, witness, record, dialogue, memory, or public interpretation before the first meaningful exchange.

Combat prose must keep Human Art, established tactics, psychology, terrain, and counterplay relevant. Do not introduce an unrelated emergency power merely to force a climax.

## Tournament Atmosphere

The tournament carries fast mythic pressure: grand entrances, meaningful titles, public stakes, sudden tactical reversals, cultural reaction, sharp humor, tenderness, dread, and irreversible consequence. Open scenes with a live pressure or question. Keep ceremony, commentary, and explanation in motion so they intensify anticipation instead of delaying the next meaningful choice or clash.

## Anti-Pattern Rules

Avoid repeated constructions, identical paragraph rhythm, overuse of rhetorical questions, constant "Not X. Not Y. Just Z." patterns, forced aphorisms, exposition disguised as dialogue, interchangeable character voices, repeated dramatic one-liners, excessive em-dash dependence, constant cliffhanger endings, and narration that overexplains what dialogue or action already showed.

Memorable lines must emerge from conflict or character truth. Do not force a quotable line into every scene.
