# Champion Presentation Checklist

Complete this before the first meaningful exchange in every battle. The presentation may be delivered through action, witnesses, records, dialogue, memory, public reaction, or opponent contrast. Do not present it as a detached biography dump.

## Identity and Stakes

- [ ] Entrance image or action shows the champion's present emotional state.
- [ ] Arrival mode is recorded and is materially different from neighboring major battles.
- [ ] Arrival mode fits the champion's identity, history, relationship to the nation, and present motive.
- [ ] Name and national entry are clear.
- [ ] Era is clear.
- [ ] Immediate reason for fighting is clear.
- [ ] The opponent's reason for fighting is also legible.

## History, Title, and Reputation

- [ ] Crown Title is named or meaningfully revealed.
- [ ] Title's historical or legendary idea is understandable.
- [ ] At least one verified accomplishment is dramatized or established.
- [ ] Disputed accomplishment or later legend is labeled as uncertain or remembered.
- [ ] The gap between person and public symbol is visible when relevant.

## Capability and Limits

- [ ] Human Art and recognizable combat method are shown.
- [ ] Crown Body specialty is legible.
- [ ] Regalia and its identity-based property are established when present.
- [ ] Legacy Trait is established when present.
- [ ] Known capability is paired with a meaningful limitation or cost.
- [ ] No ability appears without identity, history, prior observation, or fair foreshadowing.

## Contrast and Handoff

- [ ] Physical, historical, ideological, or personal contrast with the opponent is clear.
- [ ] At least one unanswered question, concealed cost, or foreshadowed danger is planted.
- [ ] The presentation pays off a prior chapter, relationship, historical, mechanical, or mystery seed.
- [ ] Both fighters receive emotional dignity, even if one is supporting the core POV.
- [ ] The final introduction beat transitions into the first meaningful exchange.

## Final Review

- [ ] The audience understands who this person was.
- [ ] The audience understands why this person is dangerous now.
- [ ] The audience understands what this person wants beyond spectacle.
- [ ] The presentation uses dramatic material rather than an information block.
- [ ] The entrance does not reuse the same procession, announcement, spotlight, narrator pattern, or first-ability reveal as the default.
